# Competition Excellence Mode

## Purpose
Optimize a competition deck for fast judge comprehension while preserving technical and evidential accuracy. This is an evaluation lens, not a claim about a specific competition's official scoring.

## Required inputs
Official rules/rubric if available; project brief and verified technical facts; evidence registry and source data; page/word/time limits; submission format and judging context. If official rules are absent, label mapping `generic_not_official`.

## Judge-facing narrative
Make explicit: problem and significance; gap in current approaches; proposed solution and specific innovation; how the system/method works; how it was tested; what evidence demonstrates; limitations, risks and maturity; impact, deployment path or next step. Do not force a fixed slide count or violate the supplied template.

## Judge-readiness checks
- Can a non-specialist judge explain the problem after the opening?
- Is the innovation a specific difference rather than an adjective?
- Is each major claim supported or marked as a proposal?
- Are baselines, controls, sample sizes, units and test conditions visible where relevant?
- Are measured results separated from targets, simulations, estimates and future plans?
- Do diagrams match the implementation actually built?
- Are limitations and unresolved risks honestly represented?
- Does the conclusion answer the original problem?
- Is the deck readable at presentation distance and in the submitted PDF?

Flag unsupported superlatives, unverified performance claims, hidden assumptions, missing baselines, ambiguous prototype maturity and diagrams implying unbuilt capabilities.

## Output
Produce rubric-to-slide mapping, judge questions, critical risks and prioritized fixes. Never predict actual scores or claim to represent official judges.
