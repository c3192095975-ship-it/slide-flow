# Design Intelligence and Aesthetic Evaluation

## Design is communication, not decoration
Every slide must have a visual reason for its layout. Consistency means a coherent design language, not repeating the same card grid on every page.

## Per-slide design brief
Record `visual_anchor`, primary/secondary/tertiary hierarchy, composition, justified density, contrast strategy, whitespace role, and source/caption treatment.

## Layout selection
Choose layout from content semantics, not a fixed template sequence. Possible compositions include full-bleed figure, split, chart-led, multi-panel, timeline, annotated image, comparison, big-number, and text-led. Vary composition when the information task changes; retain repetition when it supports comparison.

## Scientific figure grammar
- mechanism: entities, interactions, directionality, feedback and causal links;
- experiment: apparatus/context, independent/dependent variables, controls and stages;
- algorithm: inputs, transformations, model/process, outputs and feedback;
- results: real measurements, baselines, uncertainty, sample size and units;
- system: layers, interfaces, data movement and failure/feedback paths;
- comparison: same scales, explicit criteria and matched conditions;
- multi-panel: each panel has a distinct role and shared reading order.

Avoid defaulting to boxes and arrows. Use spatial structure, annotated real assets, layered cutaways, timelines, small multiples, matrices, plots, process scenes or component illustrations when they communicate the science more directly.

## Reference decomposition
Extract only transferable grid/composition, typography hierarchy, palette roles, data/figure grammar, image treatment, density/whitespace, caption/source treatment, context and license/source. Do not reproduce a complete protected page or figure without permission.

## Aesthetic review rubric (1–5)
Score message clarity/hierarchy, composition/balance, visual relevance, typography/legibility, figure quality/annotation, layout diversity, deck coherence, and audience/venue fit. A low score requires a concrete diagnosis and repair action. Scores are advisory, not official.

## Repair order
1. unsupported or incorrect content; 2. visual grammar; 3. hierarchy/composition; 4. typography/spacing/alignment; 5. color/iconography/micro-details.
